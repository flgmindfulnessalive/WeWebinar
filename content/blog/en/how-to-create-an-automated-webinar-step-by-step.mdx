Recording the presentation is the step that takes the least time. What actually decides whether your [evergreen webinar](/blog/what-is-an-evergreen-webinar) converts happens before and after the recording. Here's the full sequence.

## 1. Define a single, clear offer

Before you write a single slide, define what you're selling and to whom. A webinar that tries to sell two different things confuses the audience and lowers conversion on both. Pick one offer, one price, one call to action.

## 2. Structure the script around the moment of the offer

The structure that works best for sales webinars follows a recognizable order: the problem your audience has, why the solutions they've already tried didn't work, your method or framework, proof that it works (case studies, results), and only then the offer. Presenting the offer too early scares off someone who doesn't trust you yet; presenting it too late leaves little time to handle objections.

## 3. Record once, but record it well

You don't need a studio. You need good audio (viewers forgive the video, not the audio), a clear presentation, and a take without long cuts that break the pace. If you've already given this presentation [live](/blog/automated-webinar-vs-live-webinar) before and it worked, that recording — or an improved re-run of that same session — is your best starting point.

## 4. Choose how sessions get scheduled

You have two main formats:

- **Fixed times**: the webinar repeats at specific slots (for example, Tuesdays and Thursdays at 6pm). Works well when you want to create a sense of event.
- **Just-in-time**: the session starts a few minutes after someone registers, regardless of the time. It drastically cuts the drop-off between registration and attendance, since there's no need to wait until tomorrow to watch it.

Most businesses just starting with evergreen get better results with "just-in-time": it removes the main reason people register and then never show up.

## 5. Schedule the simulated chat

An empty, silent room feels like watching a video, not being at an event. The simulated chat — messages and frequently asked questions that appear at specific points in the video — recreates that sense of an active room. You don't need many: 8 to 12 well-placed messages, timed to where questions would genuinely come up, are enough.

## 6. Place your CTAs at the right moments, not before

A call to action that appears before you've handled the main objections gets ignored. Place your main CTA right after the proof/results section, and consider a second reminder toward the close for anyone still deciding.

## 7. Watch it start to finish before publishing

This is the step most people skip. Register for your own webinar, from start to finish, on the device your audience is most likely to use (for most, that's a phone). Confirm the chat appears when it should, the CTAs are clickable, and the video has no weird cuts.

## 8. Publish and measure the first two weeks

Once it's live, what matters is the attendance rate (how many of the people who register actually show up?), the point in the video where the most people drop off, and clicks on your main CTA. If there's a sharp drop at a specific minute, that's exactly the part of the script that needs adjusting — you don't need to re-record everything, just edit that section.

---

None of these steps require expensive tools or a production team. They require following the order: a clear offer, a script that handles objections before it sells, scheduling that reduces drop-off, and a full review before publishing.
